# Sunspindle: bounded playable experiments

Primary profile: action. This is an independent generated campaign, not an engine
combat template, and not yet a successful playtest.

The hypothesis: throwing is immediately useful, but moving before recalling changes
which enemies or obstacles the returning weight crosses. A committed ram and ranged
pressure make position consequential. Breaking the sunbird's prison opens escape;
killing every opponent is not required. The weight is held, flying, planted, or
returning, each visibly distinct. Player movement and weight flight stay local.
Hostile time slows while the player and planted/held weight are still, so thinking
and browser input latency do not consume the player's opportunity to act.

The original scope was one courtyard, two threats and one consequential breakable.
No prescribed combo, QTE chain, gear menu, decorative loot, or prewritten campaign.
Do not author more rooms to disguise an unrewarding first room. Ask privately whether
this exact room would remain worth playing if no new host content arrived. Gate
continuation on readable distinct outcomes and the player's voluntary interest,
not defeating two enemies or inspecting a clean save. The first room earned a
voluntary request for another encounter; the Threadmill did not. Its moving rear
target was fiddly, while bypassing it with two seal shots offered little decision.
Do not treat greater execution difficulty as a deeper spatial choice.

Fresh saves now open the Glassworks as a separate, short trial, not a continuation
of the stopped Threadmill save. Fixed artillery and finite blast jars change the
local spatial problem; the garden exit is a complete stopping point. A host's
normal-controls clear established feasibility, not enjoyment. Await an independent
player verdict before claiming this iteration works or adding another encounter.

The host owns implementation and audits. Give an independent player only the URL,
ordinary controls and visible premise. No tactics, hidden conditions or future room
plans. Respect a decision to stop. Record boring/unclear stretches in the verdict;
a single satisfying hit or beautiful still does not make the whole experience fun.

For continuation, react to actual player choices. Add genuinely new behavior in
this campaign's source while preserving existing progress, then activate at a safe
boundary. Already-supported room arrangements can now use `passage.stage` without
reload; see [CONTRACT.md](CONTRACT.md). Stage while useful local play remains, not
after making the player wait at a closed continuation. The earlier nine-minute
authoring gap lost momentum despite fast publication. Ordinary changes should
use focused game-owned commands where useful;
there is no universal enemy, item or room schema imposed by the engine. Do not
replace Tidelight's preserved failed-iteration ending.
