# Tidelight: generated campaign, not an engine template

A small boat explores a flooded cathedral district. The first playable ecological
verb is relocating living light: rays follow lights, the boat can lift/release them,
and its silver bell sends a local water pulse. Point-to-sail and keyboard steering
remain local while a live host authors consequences and new capabilities.

This source directory is independent from Iron Cadence and Live Canvas. Its state,
renderer and commands are game-owned creative decisions, not generic engine schemas.
Do not turn these objects into a mandatory engine component catalog.

Host entry: library/workspace tools identify the session; attach an independent
consumer and wait for semantic discoveries, lifting/releasing light and bell pulses.
Preserve the save and established physical rules. Author additions in response to
what the player actually tries. Do not prewrite a whole encounter chain or explain
all interactions in a dashboard. `world.change` can place new creatures/lights or
brief diegetic cues without remounting; new executable behaviors require source
publication guarded by the current release. Game commands use observed world
versions and the current director turn to reject stale intentions.

Pure player review: the player sees only the world, controls and a brief premise.
Never coach the hidden solution or measure enjoyment by successful completion.

Persisted regions live inside the game save. Source publications must preserve
phase/timer, pause, hull, carried lights and region state. Emit semantic changes,
not every animation/attack phase. The optional shell encounter currently supports
bell interaction and physical collisions with the awakened ruin. Its cues must
remain in the world rather than recommended-key labels. The independent player's
latest verdict called the combat repetitive; do not treat shell completion as a
fun gate or force the player through it to see the next creation.
